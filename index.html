Understanding Different Barcode Formats

Barcode formats are designed for different types of information and applications. Choosing the right format is important because not every scanner or inventory system supports every barcode type.

Code 128

Code 128 is a linear barcode format that can encode letters, numbers, and certain special characters. It is commonly used in logistics, shipping, inventory management, and internal product identification. Its suitability depends on the requirements of the receiving system.

Code 39

Code 39 is a linear barcode format used in applications such as inventory tracking, equipment identification, and industrial labeling. Its character support and data capacity differ from those of other barcode formats, so users should check whether it meets their specific requirements.

EAN and UPC Barcodes

EAN and UPC barcode formats are commonly associated with retail products. Retail identification numbers must follow the rules of the relevant numbering system. Generating a barcode image does not automatically provide an officially assigned or valid retail product identifier.

Choosing the Correct Format

Before generating a barcode, identify where it will be scanned and what information it needs to contain. Check the requirements of your inventory software, retailer, warehouse, or other receiving system. Use only a format supported by that system.

Creating many barcode images individually can be repetitive. A bulk generation workflow can reduce this manual effort by allowing users to prepare several codes in one session.

For example, a small business organizing shelves may need separate identifiers for different storage locations. A warehouse may need labels for equipment or internal inventory records. A project manager may want scannable identifiers for assets and supplies. A bulk barcode tool can support these tasks when its features match the required workflow.

Preparing Data Before Generating Barcodes

Good input data helps prevent mistakes. Before creating a batch, remove accidental spaces, check for duplicate identifiers, and confirm that each value follows the selected barcode format's requirements.

Keep a separate record of the relationship between each identifier and the item it represents. This makes it easier to verify labels and identify mistakes during inventory checks.

Avoid encoding unnecessary personal or confidential information. A barcode may be readable by anyone who has access to a compatible scanner, depending on what information it contains.

Barcode Size, Printing, and Readability

A barcode needs sufficient space and contrast to be read reliably. When preparing labels, consider the physical size of the printed code, the quality of the printer, the label material, and the capabilities of the scanner.

Avoid stretching or compressing a barcode in a way that changes its proportions. Keep the surrounding space required by the selected barcode format and avoid placing text, borders, or graphics over the encoded bars.

Always test a printed sample at its intended size before producing a large batch. A barcode that looks clear on a computer screen may not scan reliably after printing.

Common Barcode Problems and Their Solutions

The Barcode Cannot Be Scanned

Check whether the selected format is supported by the scanner. Verify the encoded value, ensure the image has enough contrast, and print a test label if the barcode will be used on physical products or equipment.

The Generated Barcode Contains Incorrect Data

Review the original input and compare it with the value returned by a barcode reader. Check for missing characters, extra spaces, duplicate entries, or formatting errors in the source list.

Some Barcodes Are Missing From a Batch

Check whether the input list contains empty rows, unsupported characters, invalid values, or entries that exceed the tool's supported limits. If an error message is displayed, follow its instructions and try generating a smaller test batch.

The Printed Barcode Is Difficult to Read

Try printing at a suitable size with clear contrast and adequate resolution. Check the label surface and printer quality, and confirm that the barcode has not been cropped or distorted.

Barcode Generation for Inventory and Asset Tracking

Barcodes can help businesses organize inventory by connecting a scannable identifier with a record in a database or inventory application. When an item is scanned, the connected system can retrieve relevant information, such as a product description, stock record, or asset entry.

A barcode image by itself does not maintain inventory records or track stock levels. Those functions require an appropriate application or database that stores and updates the associated information.

Important Information About Product Barcodes

Creating a barcode image and obtaining an officially assigned product identification number are different processes. Businesses selling products through retailers or marketplaces should check the identification requirements of the relevant organization and sales channel.

Do not assume that a randomly generated number is a valid, unique, or officially registered product identifier. Confirm that the number and barcode format meet the requirements of the intended application before using them commercially.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a bulk barcode generator used for?

It is used to create multiple barcode images for tasks such as inventory organization, asset labeling, product identification, and other workflows that use scannable identifiers.

Can every barcode be scanned by every scanner?

No. Scanner compatibility depends on the barcode format, scanner capabilities, image quality, and the data being encoded. Confirm that the intended scanner supports the selected format.

Does generating a barcode assign an official product number?

No. Generating an image does not automatically assign an officially registered product identifier. Businesses should verify the relevant numbering and retail requirements separately.

Why should barcodes be tested before printing?

Testing helps identify incorrect data, unsupported formats, poor image quality, and printing problems before many labels are produced.

Can barcodes store confidential information?

Some barcode formats can encode information that may be read by compatible scanners. Avoid including sensitive information unless the intended system has appropriate safeguards.
